Our story

A new beginning, in the place where it all started

Our story begins, a couple of times, in Ethiopia. The first beginning was the chance meeting of an academic and a nurse. They married, had a joyful baby girl, and soon had to live in different countries. Dad worked in Ethiopia while their daughter grew up nearby, cherished by her grandparents. Mom worked in the USA, raising a son as a single parent. For nearly a decade they made do with infrequent visits, until dad and daughter could join mom and son in the USA. When dad got off the plane, his son looked at him and said, "I thought dads were taller."

The family adjusted to life together and grew, welcoming a third child. This family of five was full of all the joy, laughter and everyday frustrations that come with being a family.

Then a single event took all three of their children from this world into the next, and left their parents in a sea of grief. Through it, they were surrounded by community: first strangers, then friends and family. In their grief they traveled back to Ethiopia, to where their first story started, and began a new one. A story that seeks to create hope in the darkness.

Solomon and Meseret now offer a future, the future no longer available to their own children, to the vulnerable and neglected children of the world.

Our mission

The BCE Foundation for Kids was formed in honor and memory of Blen, Christian and Emnet. We are committed to providing access to education for vulnerable and neglected children living in poverty. We seek to cultivate lifelong learning in a clean, safe and nurturing environment, by improving educational infrastructure and providing educational materials and supplies.

Our vision

A global community where all children, regardless of socioeconomic status, have equal access to education. Inspired by Blen, Christian and Emnet, we envision a prosperous Ethiopia where all children and youth have meaningful opportunities to grow and learn, in communities that offer quality education and a better future.

Current project

Atse-Fasil public elementary school

Gondar, in northwest Ethiopia, is home to around a quarter of a million people. In 2018, BCE's founding members began working with Atse-Fasil public elementary school, named after Emperor Fasilides, who ruled Ethiopia from 1632 to 1667. On the outskirts of the city, it is one of the oldest schools in the Amhara region, and it serves 3,600 students from kindergarten to 8th grade.

Although the school is publicly funded, students must buy their own notebooks, pencils, backpacks and uniforms. Many children are so poor that they spend the school day hungry. Classrooms are so crowded and understaffed that teachers rotate groups of students between recess and instruction throughout the day. The library and laboratory are too small for the materials students need, and the buildings are crumbling.
